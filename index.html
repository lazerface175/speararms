How a transfer works

Sending or receiving a firearm? Here’s the whole process, start to finish — usually faster than you’d expect.

  1. 1

    Send us the details

    Fill out the transfer request below or call us. Tell us what’s coming, where it’s coming from, and when you need it.

  2. 2

    Verify & log

    We confirm your ID, log the inbound item in our bound book, and verify the sending FFL’s license is current.

  3. 3

    NICS check

    You complete ATF Form 4473 in person. We run the NICS background check — most come back in minutes.

  4. 4

    Pick up & go

    Once approved, the firearm is yours. Outbound transfers ship tracked and insured to your receiving FFL.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to bring for a transfer?

A valid government-issued photo ID (driver’s license or passport), proof of address if your ID doesn’t match your current address, and payment for the firearm plus our transfer fee. You’ll complete ATF Form 4473 in person — it takes about 10 minutes.

How long does the background check take?

NICS checks often return within minutes, but the FBI advises it can take up to three business days. If your check comes back “delayed,” federal law requires us to wait — we’ll contact you the moment it clears.

Can I have a firearm shipped to you from another state?

Yes — that’s exactly what we’re here for. The seller must ship to a licensed dealer, never directly to you. Use our transfer request form above so we can coordinate with the sending FFL.

Do you ship firearms to my door?

Federal law requires firearms to go through a licensed dealer in your state, so we can’t ship a gun straight to your house. What we can do is arrange the transfer with a dealer near you — just ask.

How do NFA items like suppressors work?

You file an ATF Form 4 (with fingerprints, photos, and the $200 tax), wait for approval, then take possession. Trusts are popular because they simplify transfers to family members later. We’ll handle the dealer side and guide you through each step.

What if my background check is denied?

If NICS returns a denial, the firearm cannot be transferred to you. You have the right to request the reason from the FBI and to correct errors — we can point you to the appeal process.

Do you buy firearms or take trades?

We buy firearms outright and accept trades toward purchases, subject to condition, legality, and current demand. Bring the gun in (unloaded, cased) and we’ll make you an offer on the spot.

What are your hours?

Transfer pickups are by appointment only so we can give you proper attention. Build and finishing work is scheduled separately. See the contact section for current hours, or call ahead.
